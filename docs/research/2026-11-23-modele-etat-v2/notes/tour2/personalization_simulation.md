# Personalisation from in-app check-ins: simulated learning curves on EmoPhone D-1 + D-2

All numbers below are aggregates over participants and folds produced by scripts written for this round (nothing inside the repo was modified; D-3 was never opened). Abbreviations used throughout:

- **Labels.** `L_full` = the repo label (`arousal_high` = answer > the person's full-period mean; uses the future). `L_past` = app-realistic label: answer > mean of the same person's ESM answers in the previous 14 days (strictly earlier, at least 5 answers, otherwise the row is dropped). Check-ins and targets always use the same definition, and population models are retrained under it.
- **Methods.** `pop` = current 13-feature LR (§6.3 pipeline). `m1` = per-user intercept offset, MAP with N(0, τ²) prior, all past check-ins weighted equally. `m1d` (added) = same offset but old check-ins are forgotten (likelihood weight exp(−age/T), age relative to the latest check-in). `m2` = per-user Bayesian LR, prior mean = population coefficients, prior std τ_b (intercept) and τ_w (13 standardized slopes), MAP. `m3` = population LR on 13 features + 6 label-history features, trained on training participants with the same simulated check-in rate. `m4a` = m2-style Bayesian deviation on top of m3. `m4b` = logit average (1−α)·m3 + α·m2 with α = n/(n+n0). `m4c` (added) = forgetting intercept (m1d) on top of m3. `m5` = history features only; `m5t` = history + time of day/weekend (no watch). `past-mean` = Laplace mean of past check-in answers (analogue of the EmoPhone paper's "mean of past answers" baseline).
- **Metrics.** "Pooled" AUROC = all test rows of all participants together (3 check-in sampling seeds pooled). "Within-person" AUROC = AUROC computed inside each (participant, seed), averaged over seeds then participants (≥10 rows and both classes). `*`/CI = 95 % cluster bootstrap over participants (400 resamples) for pooled AUROC and Brier deltas; t-interval over participants for within-person deltas.
- **Main setting.** Arousal, 2 check-ins per day, `L_past`, rolling past-only prediction, 191 participants, 5 outer GroupKFold splits of `ml/train.py`.

## Q1. How was the simulation run, and does it reproduce the current baseline?

### Takeaway
The simulation follows the requested protocol with five documented additions (a forgetting intercept, a forgetting-intercept-on-history combination, a fixed-row "frozen" design, a time-shuffle null and a "gap" experiment). With the repo label it reproduces the repo's cross-validated AUROC exactly (0.563 per-fold mean), so the population baseline is the same model the app ships.

### Inferences
- Additions and why: (a) `m1d` and `m4c` were added after a first run showed that the frozen per-person offset carries little information weeks later, so "forgetting" had to be tested explicitly; (b) a **frozen design** (first n check-ins, then nothing; evaluated on fixed rows on days ≥ 21) gives learning curves on the same rows for every n and isolates the intercept-invariance property; (c) a **time-shuffle null** (each person's (features, label) pairs permuted across their own time slots) separates "learning the person's base rate" from "tracking the person's current state" and reveals an evaluation artefact; (d) a **gap experiment** measures what is left when the latest answer is 1–7 days old; (e) bucket metrics use rows bucketed by the number of check-ins strictly before them (0, 1–4, 5–9, 10–19, 20–39, 40–59, 60+) or by day of use (1, 2–3, 4–7, 8–14, 15–21, 22–28, 29+); method comparisons are always on identical rows.
- The check-in is simulated as the binary label itself (one tap "more / less than usual"), not the −3..+3 value; a richer answer could only add information.

### Gaps
- The simulated check-in is an ESM answer re-coded relative to a computed "usual"; a real user answering "more or less energy than usual?" anchors on an unknown internal reference. Neither `L_full` nor `L_past` is that reference; they bracket it.
- Random-within-day timing approximates app usage only roughly (EmoPhone prompts were scheduled by the study, not chosen by the user).

## Q2. How much does prediction improve as the user answers (arousal, 2 check-ins/day)?

### Takeaway
Clearly and quickly, but not on day 1: with app-realistic labels the best methods go from pooled AUROC 0.555 (population model) to 0.666 over the period of use, and from ≈ 0.55–0.57 to ≈ 0.69–0.70 from the third week on; gains are reliable from about 5–9 check-ins and the 4th day of use. Within-person ranking gains are smaller (0.560 → 0.596) and calibration improves (Brier 0.246 → 0.227).

### Inferences
- The order of magnitude matches the EmoPhone paper's label-history baseline (≈ 0.69 pooled after 2+ weeks here at only 2 answers/day, binary answers, no future-based label), which makes the result plausible.
- Day 1 (and in practice days 1–3) is the population model or marginally worse; the honest product message is "after a few days of answering, the queue fits you better", not "better from the first answer". The previous report's estimate ("< 0.02 AUROC in 2 weeks" for a per-person intercept) was too pessimistic for a forgetting offset under rolling updates, but right for a frozen/static offset (see Q4).
- Most of the pooled-AUROC gain is calibration-like: personal probabilities spread from a ±0.06 band around 0.46 to roughly 0.28–0.65, which is what lets a fixed threshold switch between "calming" and "energising" queues.

### Gaps
- The later-week rise (≈ +0.03 between week 1 and week 3 for m1d, whose memory is only ~2 days) seems to reflect the EmoPhone timeline (states more persistent or further from "usual" later in the study, and the 14-day `L_past` baseline maturing), so it may not transfer to app users; no direct test was possible.
- Confidence intervals treat folds and seeds as part of one pool; they ignore the variance from re-tuning hyper-parameters.

## Q3. Which learning method should be used, per budget? (incl. the intercept-only limitation)

### Takeaway
Forgetting matters more than model richness: a one-parameter personal offset that forgets answers older than ~2 days (m1d) is as good as or better than every richer method at every budget for arousal, and the history-feature model (m3) ties it; per-user slopes (m2), Bayesian deviations on top of m3 (m4a), logit averaging (m4b) and stacking a forgetting offset on m3 (m4c) add ≤ 0.003 AUROC. A static (non-forgetting) intercept improves pooled AUROC but, as expected, not within-person ranking.

### Inferences
- Recommended method: **m1d** (implemented as `m1d_now`, see Q7) — the shipped Core ML model unchanged + a per-user logit offset b, prior N(0, 1.0²), fitted by a 1-D Newton MAP on check-ins weighted by exp(−age/2 days). Day 1 = population model exactly; no change to the §6 feature contract, no retraining, no new golden fixtures; one number stored on the phone. It is the best or tied-best method at every budget for arousal and needs only check-in timestamps and answers.
- m3 is the alternative if the team accepts changing the feature contract (6 new inputs, retrain, new fixtures): it ties m1d on AUROC, is slightly better within-person, and degrades more gracefully when answers stop (Q7). Combining them (m4c) does not pay.
- Per-user slopes (m2/m4a) are not worth the complexity: +0.002–0.003 AUROC after weeks.
- Because intercept-only personalisation cannot change the ranking of a person's own moments, any claim of "the AI learns how *your* body reacts" would be unsupported by these data; what is learned is "how you have been lately".

### Gaps
- The best T (≈ 2 days for `L_past`, ≈ 4 days for `L_full`/1 per day) was tuned on EmoPhone students; real users' state persistence may differ.
- Only linear/logistic learners were tested; no tree or sequence model.

## Q4. Is the gain real or an artefact of the label definition / evaluation?

### Takeaway
Mostly real, with two caveats. The full-period label leaks a little (gains appear 1–2 days earlier and are ≈ 0.01 higher than with the app-realistic past-only label), but the past-only label keeps most of the gain. The time-shuffle null shows that about half of the pooled gain is plain "base-rate" learning and the rest comes from temporal persistence of the person's state; and a frozen offset learned early does not transfer to week 3+, so the benefit lives in recent answers, not in accumulated knowledge of the person.

### Inferences
- Leakage mechanism of `L_full`: the threshold is the mean of all the person's answers, including future ones. Past labels then carry information about the future through the threshold (if early answers sit above the full-period mean, later ones must on average sit below it, and any study-long drift shows up as long runs of identical labels). History features and personal offsets — and population models trained on training participants built the same way — can exploit this; the app cannot. `L_past` removes it (the app question is about "usual", which can only be based on the past). The small `L_full` − `L_past` gap (≈ 0.01–0.025 AUROC, 1–2 days earlier significance) bounds the leakage; the `L_past` numbers should be the ones reported.
- `L_past` has its own mechanical effect: a high answer raises the 14-day baseline, making the next labels a little more likely to be "less than usual". A real user anchoring "usual" on recent days would produce the same effect, so it is not leakage, but it weakens day 1–3 results.
- The within-window artefact is a finite-sample effect: inside a short window with a fixed set of labels, a predictor that rises after "high" answers is mechanically anti-correlated with the remaining labels (sampling without replacement). It biases within-person AUROC *against* adaptive methods, so the real within-person gains (+0.036 for m1d, +0.038 for m3) are, if anything, under-estimates; relative to the null (−0.028) the temporal signal is worth ≈ +0.06. Per-bucket within-person AUROC should not be used to judge methods; whole-period within-person AUROC, pooled AUROC and Brier should.
- Decomposition (arousal `L_past` 2/day, pooled AUROC): pop 0.555 → ≈ 0.62 from learning each person's base rate (null; what static m1/m2 achieve on real data: 0.624/0.627) → ≈ 0.66–0.67 when the method also follows recent state (m1d/m3). Because the frozen real-data curve is flat while the null one rises, the persistent part of a person's base rate is not learnable from the first 1–2 weeks in EmoPhone: personal "levels" drift over weeks.

### Gaps
- A third, possibly more realistic label ("usual" = a fixed personal reference from an onboarding week) was not simulated.
- The null preserves base rates but also destroys within-day time-of-day alignment between labels and the clock-based history feature; this slightly understates history methods in the null.

## Q7. What happens when the user stops answering, and does asking at uncertain moments help?

### Takeaway
The gain fades within days without new answers: for the best methods about 30–40 % of the pooled-AUROC gain is gone after 2 days, about half after 4 days and roughly three quarters after a week. ## Q8. What does this imply for the proposal?

### Takeaway
The evidence supports a small, honest feature: a one-tap relative check-in ~2 times a day (optionally a second pleasantness tap), and a single personal offset with forgetting added to the existing Core ML output, starting at 0 on day 1. Expected effect, if EmoPhone students are representative: no change on day 1, a reliable improvement from about day 4 (≈ 5–10 answers), and pooled AUROC around 0.66–0.70 for arousal (≈ 0.70+ for valence) while the user keeps answering.

- Implementation cost of m1d (in its `m1d_now` form) in Swift: store (timestamp, answer, population logit at that time) triples on the phone (health-adjacent data stay local), and at prediction time t solve a 1-D convex problem for b — minimise Σ_i exp(−(t − t_i)/T)·logloss(y_i, σ(logit_i + b)) + b²/(2·1.0²) with a few Newton steps — then p = σ(logit_pop(t) + b). - Claims for the report: "after about four days of answering twice a day, the model's probabilities separate the user's higher- and lower-energy moments better (simulation on EmoPhone: pooled AUROC 0.56 → ≈ 0.67 over the period of use)"; avoid "learns your body" (within-person sensor weights do not improve) and avoid any emotion naming.
- Before the course deadline the safest use is to collect check-ins during the pilot without changing predictions, then evaluate m1d offline on the pilot data (same rolling protocol), as the A/B test needs a frozen model.

### Gaps
- No validation on D-3 (reserved) or on Apple Watch users; EmoPhone used Fitbit-era data and students in Korea.
- The real answer rate in the app (and its drop-off over weeks) is unknown and drives the benefit (Q7).
